Cross Validation Case Study
===========================

Two case studies of the cross-validation procedure were performed – examining Seattle, WA and Golden, CO. For both cases, a small amount of OpenStreetMap greenspace could not be cross validated as greenspace according to the ESA data. 
*Golden, CO* 

-	2% of the total greenspace could not be cross validated. 
-	A basic visual check was performed on the non-cross-validated greenspace areas. According to this check, a majority of the areas were small in size, located in close proximity to residential and other built-up areas. These areas consisted of sidewalk berms, courtyards, and other such greenspaces. 
-	These areas most likely cannot be cross validated because of their size and proximity to built-up areas. Due to the resolution of the ESA raster data (10-meters), it cannot obtain level of granularity required to mark those locations as greenspaces. 

*Seattle, WA*

-	1% of the total greenspace could not be cross validated
-	Like the Golden, CO case, according to a visual check of non-cross-validated areas, most of them were small and near built-up areas
-	Again, these areas most likely cannot be cross validated because their small size exceeds the granularity of the ESA raster data.

Overall, for these two cases, only a very small percentage of greenspace could not be cross validated. Therefore, it can be argued that the ESA WorldCover cross validation process is extremely effective.
